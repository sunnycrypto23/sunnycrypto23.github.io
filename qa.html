Q&A — GST 112 Nigerian People and Culture

Below are 20 practice questions drawn directly from the full course summary. Click each question to reveal the answer. If you have a follow-up question, use the social links or email at the bottom.

Module 1 – Pre‑Colonial Nigeria

1. How was the Yoruba Oyo Empire governed? Name the three main institutions.

The Alafin (king), the Oyo‑Mesi (council of chiefs headed by the Bashorun), and the Ogboni cult (religious‑political body enforcing customary law). The system had checks and balances.

2. What made the Igbo political system different from the Hausa and Yoruba?

The Igbo were largely stateless/decentralized — no kings or central empire. Governance was through age‑grades, titled men (merit‑based ozo title), village councils, and the earth deity Ala.

Module 2 – Culture

3. Define culture and list three of its functions.

Culture is the totality of a people's way of life (beliefs, values, norms, language, art, technology, food, dress, religion, social institutions). Functions: identity, social control, unity/cohesion, continuity, adaptation.

4. Distinguish between material and non‑material culture with examples.

Material culture: tangible objects (tools, clothing, buildings, art, food). Non‑material culture: intangible ideas (beliefs, values, language, norms, religion).

Module 3 – Heritage & Cultural Policy

5. Name three sources of Nigerian heritage.

Oral tradition (folktales, proverbs, myths), archaeology/artifacts (Nok terracotta, Benin bronzes, Ife heads), written records (colonial accounts), religion/festivals, and institutions (chieftaincy, age‑grades).

6. What are the main challenges to preserving Nigerian heritage?

Westernization/globalization, religious conflict labelling traditions as fetish, loss of elder custodians, looting/theft (Benin Bronzes abroad), inadequate funding, rural‑urban migration.

Module 4 – Colonial Rule

7. Explain Indirect Rule and why it failed in Eastern Nigeria.

Indirect Rule governed through existing traditional rulers. In the East, the Igbo had no centralized chiefs, so the British appointed “warrant chiefs” with no traditional legitimacy, causing resentment (e.g., Aba Women's Riots).

8. What was the significance of the 1914 Amalgamation?

It merged the Northern and Southern Protectorates into one Nigeria for administrative/economic convenience. It is widely seen as the root of later ethnic/regional tensions because it was not based on shared identity.

Module 5 – Nationalism & Independence

9. Who is called the “father of Nigerian nationalism”?

Herbert Macaulay, founder of the NNDP.

10. Name the three major political parties at independence and their leaders.

NCNC – Nnamdi Azikiwe (East); Action Group (AG) – Obafemi Awolowo (West); NPC – Ahmadu Bello (North).

Module 6 – Nation‑Building Challenges

11. What triggered the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970)?

After the January 1966 coup (mostly Igbo officers) and the July 1966 counter‑coup, massacres of Igbo in the North led to the Eastern Region declaring the Republic of Biafra under Ojukwu. The federal government fought to keep Nigeria one.

12. What was Gowon's reconciliation policy after the war?

“No victor, no vanquished.”

Module 7 – Organs of Government

13. List the hierarchy of Nigerian courts from lowest to highest.

Magistrate/District Courts → High Courts (State/Federal) → Court of Appeal → Supreme Court. Specialized courts: Sharia and Customary Courts of Appeal.

14. State two functions of the judiciary.

Interpreting the constitution/laws; settling disputes; protecting human rights; judicial review; upholding rule of law.

Module 8 – Citizenship

15. List three ways Nigerian citizenship can be acquired.

By birth (descent), by registration (e.g., spouse of a Nigerian), by naturalization (residency, good character, livelihood).

16. What is the “indigene vs. settler” problem?

A person born and raised in a state may be treated as an outsider for jobs, admissions, or appointments because their ethnic origin is elsewhere, leading to discrimination and conflict.

Module 9 – Ethnicity & National Integration

17. What is the Federal Character Principle?

Federal appointments and admissions must reflect Nigeria's diversity, aiming to prevent domination by any one ethnic group. It is debated between inclusion and merit.

18. How does NYSC promote national integration?

Graduates serve one year outside their home state, building cross‑cultural bonds. Limitations include language barriers, poor amenities, and insecurity.

Module 10 – National Ethics & Re‑orientation

19. Name three agencies or campaigns created to re‑orient Nigerian values.

WAI (War Against Indiscipline), MAMSER, NOA (National Orientation Agency), EFCC, ICPC.

Module 11 – Trade & Self‑Reliance

20. What is the Igba‑boi apprenticeship system?

A young person works under a master trader for years and is then “settled” with capital or goods to start their own business. It is a homegrown model of entrepreneurship and skill transfer.
